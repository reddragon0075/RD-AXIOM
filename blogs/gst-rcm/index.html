GST SAC 9966 & RCM: The Corporate Guide to Tax Splits, ITC Restrictions, and Dual-Ledger Billing

A technical breakdown of GST SAC 996601 Reverse Charge Mechanism rules, Section 17(5) 13-seat Input Tax Credit restrictions, and automated dual-ledger billing.

Corporate travel desks and finance controllers manage complex tax splits across employee transportation contracts. Under Indian GST law, passenger transport services fall under SAC 996601, where tax liability shifts dynamically based on vendor corporate status, vehicle seating capacity, and fuel inclusion terms.

Misclassifying a vendor as forward-charge when Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) applies creates statutory tax exposure during annual departmental audits.

1. When Does Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) Apply Under SAC 996601?

Under Notification No. 29/2019-Central Tax (Rate), RCM applies to passenger transport services when two conditions meet:

  1. The service supplier is an individual, partnership firm, or unregistered enterprise billing at the 5% concessional GST rate without Input Tax Credit.
  2. The service recipient is a registered corporate body corporate.

When an enterprise hires sedans or SUVs from non-corporate fleet proprietors at 5% GST, the corporate recipient must pay the 5% tax directly under RCM through electronic cash ledgers. If the fleet supplier is a registered private limited entity billing at 12% forward charge with full ITC, the supplier collects and deposits the tax directly.

2. The 13-Seat Input Tax Credit Rule Under Section 17(5)

Section 17(5)(a) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act blocks Input Tax Credit on passenger motor vehicles with approved seating capacity of 13 persons or fewer (including the driver).

There are two statutory exceptions where corporate enterprises can legally claim ITC on passenger vehicles:

  • Obligatory Statutory Transport: Transportation provided to employees under statutory labor laws (such as mandatory night-shift drop services for female employees under state Shops & Establishment rules).
  • Sub-Contracted Fleet Re-Supply: Corporate car rental agencies hiring associate cabs for outward commercial passenger transport.

In ordinary executive car rental duties, ITC remains blocked for corporate recipients.

3. Dual-Ledger Financial Math: Stopping Negative Margin Leaks

Commercial car rental operators manage two separate financial realities on every trip:

  1. Client Gross Receivable: What the corporate enterprise owes based on negotiated corporate rate cards and fuel index escalations.
  2. Supplier Net Payable: What the associate vendor or driver partner earns after platform fee splits, toll reconciliations, and advance payments.
