Garage-to-Garage (G2G) vs. Point-to-Point (P2P) Billing: Solving the Deadhead Dispute

A financial and operational comparison between Garage-to-Garage and Point-to-Point billing models in corporate car rental contracts.

Deadhead mileage, the distance a commercial vehicle travels between the operating garage and the passenger's pickup location, is the single most contentious line item in corporate car rental invoices. Corporate travel desks seek Point-to-Point (P2P) billing to pay only for passenger ride time, while fleet operators require Garage-to-Garage (G2G) compensation to cover non-revenue fuel and driver time.

Choosing the wrong billing model or failing to establish transparent deadhead constraints creates constant invoice disputes and margin leakage.

1. Comparing G2G and P2P Accounting Models

Understanding the structural differences between these two billing conventions defines corporate procurement strategy:

DimensionGarage-to-Garage (G2G)Point-to-Point (P2P)
Start PointVehicle leaves commercial garage / hub.Passenger boards at designated pickup geofence.
End PointVehicle returns to commercial garage / hub.Passenger disembarks at destination geofence.
Deadhead CostBilled directly to corporate client.Absorbed by vendor or priced into base package.
Ideal Use CaseFull-day dedicated rentals, outstation trips, VIP events.Local spot airport transfers, fixed-route employee shuttles.

2. The Abuse of Uncapped Deadhead Mileage

Under unrestricted G2G billing, unscrupulous fleet vendors dispatch vehicles from remote garages located 30 kilometres away from the client pickup location, inflating the total duty invoice by 60 deadhead kilometres.

To prevent procurement disputes, modern corporate contracts enforce deadhead caps:

  • Maximum Deadhead Allowance: Capping billable garage distance (such as a maximum allowance of 10 km or 30 minutes from the corporate hub).
  • Virtual Hub Geofencing: Defining a centralized virtual garage coordinate rather than the vendor's physical depot, ensuring predictable deadhead billing.
  • Auto-Slab Optimization: The tariff engine automatically evaluates whether G2G or P2P produces the most equitable rate while preserving agreed vendor gross margins.

3. Automated Geofenced Metering in Axiom Mobility

Axiom Mobility eliminates paper trip sheet disputes by tracking duty state transitions automatically through driver app GPS telemetry:

  1. Garage Exit Event: When the vehicle leaves the designated garage geofence, the driver app records a timestamped telemetry baseline.
  2. Passenger Pickup Event: OTP validation at the passenger pickup point marks the beginning of the active passenger leg.
  3. Passenger Drop Event: Destination OTP validation closes passenger billing.
  4. Garage Return Event: Returning to the hub geofence closes the G2G duty cycle, providing an auditable, timestamped breadcrumb trail for finance verification.

4. Contractual Clarity Protects Partnerships

Establishing clear deadhead rules protects long-term supplier relationships:

  • Corporate clients eliminate unexpected invoice inflation on short-distance executive transfers.
  • Fleet vendors recover genuine vehicle mobilization expenses without resorting to hidden fees.
  • Finance teams review transparent digital vouchers showing exact passenger kilometres and capped deadhead splits.
